### The Reality Check
Lately, I’ve been wrestling with a frustrating realization: **I spend far too much time and energy carrying batch responsibilities instead of building my own future.**

I catch myself feeling bitter when I see peers who are completely self-centered—they contribute nothing to batch activities, protect their own time, and quietly get ahead. Meanwhile, I jump in to help and end up feeling drained, compromised, and questioning my own intelligence for doing it.

### The Wake-Up Calls
1. **Aswini Sir's Confrontation:**  
   He asked about my results and was blunt with me: *"Ichi, why are your grades slipping? You're losing ground."* It stung, but it forced me to face reality and sparked a much-needed fire under me.
2. **Dilenya's Repeated Warning:**  
   *"Ichi, focus on your studies instead of doing so much social work. You will lose someday if you keep this up."*

### The Psychological Trap
The real problem is the guilt. Whenever a crowd gathers to organize or fix something, I feel awful sitting it out—even when I know the effort might be disorganized, ineffective, or unnecessary for me to be there. I abandon my own work because of the pressure to be present and helpful.

### The Bottom Line
I cannot afford to keep doing this. If I don't turn things around and pull off great grades this semester, the consequences will be severe. My priority has to shift back to my own survival and success.
