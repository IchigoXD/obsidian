The Normal Approximation to the Binomial 
Distribution 
The normal distribution is sometimes introduced as a 
continuous distribution that provides a close approximation 
to the binomial distribution when n, the number of trials, is

very large and θ, the probability of a success on an

Steps to working a normal approximation to the binomial 
distribution 
1. Identify success, the probability of success, the number 
of trials, and the desired number of successes. Since this 
is a binomial problem, these are the same things which 
were identified when working a binomial problem. 
 
2. Convert the discrete x to a continuous x.

Exercise: Use the normal approximation to the binomial 
distribution to determine the probability of getting 6 heads 
and 10 tails in 16 flips of a balanced coin. 
Solution: Let X is number of heads from 16 flipping of a 
balanced coin. 
X has a binomial distribution with n=16 and θ=0.5. Hence 
the required probability is Pr(X=6). 
To approximate this probability using normal distribution, 
it is necessary to perform continuity correction. i.e. 
Pr(X=6)=Pr(5.5<X<6.5). 
Then the required probability can be evaluated using 
normal distribution with mean 𝜇 = 𝑛𝜃 = 16×0.5=8 and 
standard deviation 𝜎 = ඥ𝑛𝜃(1 −𝜃) = √16 ⋅ 0.5 ⋅ 0.5=
2.
